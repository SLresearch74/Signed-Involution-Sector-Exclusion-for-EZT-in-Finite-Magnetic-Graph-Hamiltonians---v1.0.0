# Candidate signatures and a sufficient bounded classifier

## Candidate audit

The proposed tuple (additive orders, p-cycle balance, compatible Galois data, signed Song relations, orientation, declared incidence) is not automatically sufficient. If its event-incidence component records only labels, placement and recombination membership, the tuple omits the actual physical amplitude polynomials. Even retaining every fixed Song value does not repair that omission.

If instead the incidence component includes all coefficient functions, grade/delay markings, poles and exact recombination maps, the tuple can reconstruct the finite arithmetic recurrence and is sufficient under the frozen hypotheses. That is a full marked reconstruction package, not a newly proved compression. Relative phase information may be represented by exact signed values, a relation matrix, or a sufficiently marked simultaneous Galois description; literal lists of pairwise ratios are not claimed uniquely necessary.

## Compact sufficient signature — PROVED-SYNTHESIS

Declare the class precisely: fixed prime-power oriented Hermitian circulants; certified ordered exact-dark site pair (s,l); one recombined degree-one directed rank-one event A=(a|l>+b|s>)<s|; polynomial amplitudes over the response field; fixed PID domain stratum on which (a,b) is the unit ideal; endpoint image splitting, and structured reachability rather than ordinary Kalman controllability.

For this class take the certified dark-pair/mask membership, ordered event template, stratum, and the two normalized amplitude ideal classes ([a],[b]). The reconstruction is g_gamma=normalize(a) for every fibre block and h_(delta,gamma)=normalize(b) for every declared arrow. Thus K0 and Kmax are both all blocks at a zero of a, and both zero otherwise. This follows from the all-future formula proved in the graph-realizability audit.

The certificate of class membership contains actual masks and nonzero weights; after membership is established their detailed magnitudes are irrelevant to these generators because they are fixed units. This is meaningful compression: neither the finite recurrence nor a list of all Song ratios is needed to recover g and h in this subclass.

The amplitude ideal pair is relatively necessary for the separate targets: varying [a] while b=1 changes g but not h; varying [b] while a=1 changes h but not g. This is not absolute canonical minimality, and the membership certificate itself is not proved minimal. For zero loci alone multiplicities can be discarded; for normalized generators they cannot.

## Actual amplitude-blind obstruction theorem — PROVED-SYNTHESIS

Fix the connected n=8 baseline C={1,3}, (s,l)=(0,2) and its entire static marked Song package. For arbitrary f in K8[t], the family

\[
A_f=(f(t)|2\rangle+|0\rangle)\langle0|
\]

has constant rank-one coefficient and projected images on all t, with g_gamma=normalize(f) and h_e=1. The family (|2>+f(t)|0>)<0| instead has g_gamma=1 and h_e=normalize(f). These statements include zero and constant f.

Consequently any proposed invariant that is unchanged under these amplitude changes cannot determine g or h. This kills amplitude-blind signatures built from fibre sizes, static support, additive orders, p-cycle classes, any static Galois data, local zero patterns, unsigned magnitudes, and even exact signed *Song* relations plus unweighted declared incidence. Arbitrary prescribed roots and degrees can occur without changing the baseline or the strong architecture. The affine pairs f=t-1 and f=t-2 already give distinct physical loci; the marked parameter coordinate is fixed and translations are not identified.

This does not prove that every possible compressed signature fails. The amplitude-ideal signature above survives. The full general multi-event compact-taxonomy question remains OPEN, while the stated amplitude-blind impossibility result is complete.
