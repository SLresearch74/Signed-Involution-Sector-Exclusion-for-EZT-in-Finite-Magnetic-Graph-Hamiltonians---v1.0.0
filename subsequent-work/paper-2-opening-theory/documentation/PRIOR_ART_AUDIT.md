# Prior-art audit

Search date: **3 August 2026**. This audit is claim-level and conservative. A failure to locate an anticipatory theorem is recorded as absence in the searched sources, not proof of global priority.

## 1. Dark states and coherent population trapping

Aspect et al. (1988) establish coherent population trapping as a physical dark-state mechanism, and Gaubatz et al. (1990) establish stimulated Raman adiabatic passage. Zhao, Kuang and Liao (2026) give a general arrowhead/bipartite-network theory for dark states in arbitrary multilevel coupling configurations. These sources motivate the physical language and broad dark-state structure. They do not state the M3A six-state numerator, its two dark planes, the perturbation/time trichotomy or the coefficient-sensitive pullback atlas.

## 2. Zero transfer and quantum walks

Godsil (2012) studies structural restrictions for perfect state transfer. Sett et al. (2019) study zero transfer in chiral continuous-time quantum walks, and Zimborás et al. (2013) study transport enhancement under time-reversal symmetry breaking. This literature overlaps at transfer suppression and interference. The present theorem is narrower in model and broader in local coefficient geometry; it does not claim to subsume quantum-walk zero-transfer classifications.

## 3. Realisation, Markov parameters and transfer functions

Ho and Kalman (1966) connect input/output data to state-space realisations. Noferini et al. (2023) develop structured perturbation theory for transfer-function matrices and their zeros. These works make resolvent and moment methods standard tools. The M3A contribution is the exact coefficient ideal, endpoint access and Newton/Gröbner synthesis for one locked entry, not a new general theory of transfer matrices.

## 4. Noncommutative word series and weighted automata

Fliess (1981), Berstel and Reutenauer (2010), and Sakarovitch (2009) provide general languages for noncommutative/rational series and recognisable weighted series. The endpoint-access theorem is compatible with that framework. Its model-specific depth function, compatible endpoint pair and sharp boundary sum are presented as an exact special synthesis, not as replacing the general word-series literature.

## 5. Newton polyhedra and tropical initial forms

Kouchnirenko (1976) and Varchenko (1976) are foundational Newton-polyhedron sources. Maclagan and Sturmfels (2015) give the modern tropical/initial-form framework; Sturmfels and Tevelev (2008) treat tropical elimination. The standard operation “expose a face by a weight” comes from this body of work. What is specific here is the four-vertex M3A polyhedron, the 15 positive cells, and the proof that moment and amplitude face-zero loci can differ after a time-depth collision.

## 6. Scheme-theoretic tropicalisation

Giansiracusa and Giansiracusa (2016) show that equations, not only point sets, matter in tropicalisation. This supports the paper's caution that support data alone do not retain all coefficient information. It does not state the PB1 aggregation formula or the exact M3A marked-section hierarchy.

## 7. Gröbner fans and monomial degenerations

Fukuda, Jensen and Thomas (2007) give algorithms for Gröbner fans, while Miller and Sturmfels (2005) provide the commutative-algebra background for monomial ideals, Hilbert series and resolutions. These are the standard tools behind GF1. The 16 cones, the twelve/four radical split and the particular doubled planes are exact computations for `(L,Q)` in the locked coordinates.

## 8. Rees algebras, blow-ups and contact loci

Rees algebras and blow-ups are standard constructions; Ein, Lazarsfeld and Mustaţă (2004) give a major arc-space/contact-locus framework. The M3A contact profile and blow-up charts are specialisations. The paper does not claim the construction itself, nor a canonical multi-Rees physical meaning.

## 9. Noetherianity and coefficient ideals

Finite generation of ideals in polynomial rings is standard Noetherian algebra (Atiyah and Macdonald, 1969). The fixed-weight theorem applies that standard result after finite ramification to the coefficient sequence of this pulled amplitude. Novelty, if any, lies in the application and scope correction, not in Noetherianity.

## 10. D-finite and rational series

Stanley (1980) is a standard source for D-finite power series, but the paper does not require a D-finite closure theorem. The finite-weight result uses coefficient-ideal Noetherianity, not a claim that every multi-scale amplitude belongs to a new D-finite class.
